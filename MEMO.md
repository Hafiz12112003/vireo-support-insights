**Re:** Why CSAT fell, and why I would not spend the Rs 4 L on the bottom ten yet

**The short answer.** CSAT fell mainly because of one batch of Pulse 2 earbuds, not because of your agents.

**What happened.** Three production lots (PL2-2510, -2511, -2512, about 1,980 orders) have a charging fault, usually the left earbud. On those lots about 38% of tickets end in a replacement against 11% on other Pulse 2 lots, and CSAT on them is 2.7 against 3.5. Take them out and CSAT stays around 3.3 to 3.4 through the worst months; with them in, it drops to 2.95 in February. The excess so far is about **Rs 16 L**: roughly 710 extra replacements at Rs 1,820 each (unit cost plus Rs 340, per your policy; Arjun's Rs 2,500 is too high for this product) and about 1,040 extra contacts.

**Your bottom ten.** I built the dashboard you asked for. But six of the ten are the Tier 2 warranty team, and four are the chat hardware rota. They get the angry Pulse 2 tickets by design, which is what Neha said. Once I compare each agent with what you would expect for the tickets they handled, Tier 2 is within normal noise, bar one borderline case (and your policy says not to compare them with Tier 1). The four chat agents still sit 0.24 to 0.33 points below expectation, mostly during the Pulse 2 months. They share one queue, so I cannot tell whether that is the people or the queue. Retraining them now could be money spent on the wrong cause. The same applies to the Diwali top five: a raw CSAT ranking rewards easy queues.

**What I recommend.**
1. Ask Quality and the supplier about the three lots now, and hold remaining Pulse 2 stock from those and later batches until checked.
2. Hold the Rs 4 L. Spend two weeks checking how the chat rota is assigned. If the four still trail on ordinary tickets, coach them. If not, none of the budget is needed for them.
3. Switch on the weekly alert. On this history it would have flagged Pulse 2 in the week of 1 December 2025. Holding each lot two weeks after its own test fired would have saved about Rs 5 L; holding the flagged lot and later-made ones from two weeks after that first alert, up to about Rs 12.6 L (that assumes you can swap in good stock).

**Also noticed.** SLA breaches cost Rs 3.7 L in credits and are 13% on the night shift against 8% on days; the helpdesk reports them against the resolving agent, which blames the wrong person for a coverage gap.

**How sure I am.** The Pulse 2 finding is strong: every sub-lot shows it and no other lot does. The agent findings are weaker: only about 45% of customers answer the survey, and 8% of tickets have no lot code. The export has about 150 tickets a week, not the 650 you mention, so these figures are for this export and are not scaled up.
